What makes a useful comparison?

  • Compare the same estate, layout, saleable area, floor and condition.
  • Use completed sales to assess asking prices; a discount to an estate average alone is not a bargain.
  • Check whether a listing is repeated, stale, subsidised, premium-unpaid or for a partial interest.
  • For renting, compare all-in monthly cost, commute and break terms.
  • For buying, inspect major works, financing and estimated net cash flow.

Evidence & methodology

Keep your tracker current

Weekly source checks + optional local observations

The public dataset is checked weekly. Each component retains its own successful source date. The original 90-day estate summary and historical unit examples remain separately dated; a weekly run never makes old observations look new.

Export your observations, then import a newer CSV with the same columns. Repeat observations build an estate-level rent and sale proxy history in this browser. Local history is not quality-adjusted and cannot establish a district-wide index.

What you won’t find here

No invented local rental trends, no matched-unit returns inferred from different flats, and no promise about future prices. All 18 districts are listed; only sampled neighbourhoods have numbers.
